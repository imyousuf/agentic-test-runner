Test: A reader can open the newest post from the front page

Why this matters:
The front page is the only route most readers take into the blog. If the post
links stop resolving, every article is unreachable even though each one still
exists at its own URL.

Prerequisites:
- The blog is published and the front page lists at least one post

Steps:
1. Open the front page and follow the link for the first post listed
2. Confirm the reader has landed on the post itself

Expected Results:
- The browser is no longer on the front page: the URL contains a post path
- The page shows the post's own title as a heading
- The post's body text is on the page, not just its summary
- The site header linking back to the blog home is still present

Notes for the compiler:
- This is a Hugo blog with no data-testid attributes anywhere. Use the
  structural selectors that are actually there.
- On the front page the whole card is wrapped in <a class="post-link">, which
  contains the date, an h3 with the title, and the summary. The link is
  a.post-link — there is no anchor nested inside the heading.
- On a post page the title is the h1, and the body is in .post-content.
- Do not assert an exact post title: the newest post changes when the author
  publishes. Assert the shape instead — that a title exists and that the same
  text appears in the page heading.
